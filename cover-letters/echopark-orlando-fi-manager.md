## Why this letter is built the way it is

**Relocation goes first, and it is a strength.** His son, daughter-in-law, and
granddaughter are in the Orlando area and he wants the move regardless of the
job. Every hiring manager reading an out-of-market application is asking "will
this person leave in six months?" — answering it in the first paragraph, with a
reason that has nothing to do with them, removes the objection permanently.

**The coaching is the lead, because they named the job after it.** They call it
"Finance Coach," and the responsibility list is half sales-floor development:
forecast with each salesperson, coach them, run sales meetings, provide
on-the-job training, review productivity with the Dealer. That is exactly what
Santos did with a 15-person floor for 28 years — and it is the part of his
record every other application has underused.

**The Nissan awards are framed as performance, not pedigree.** EchoPark is a
used-vehicle, one-price brand with no manufacturer product line. So the letter
says "different brand, different model — but the performance behind those
awards is what I would bring," rather than trading on Nissan credentials that
don't apply here.

**Bilingual gets a line.** Orlando's market makes it material, same as Miami.

**The close is the opening-team argument.** A store opening in December needs a
finance office that works in January. A 30-year operator is a lower-risk
opening hire than someone learning the job.
