Captured 2026-09-07 from the LinkedIn posting (job 4462064909), pasted by Juan.
Posted ~6 days prior, 10 applicants. On-site, full-time.
**Brand new dealership, opening December 2026** — this is an opening-team hire.

## Comp
Stated average annual income potential **$90K–$110K**. Health/dental/vision,
401(k) with 4% match, paid vacation, casual dress.

## The role, in their words
The Finance Manager — "**Finance Coach**" — ensures the dealership meets
performance targets by selecting, securing, and offering the best financial
products for profit performance and guest satisfaction. Wants strong financial
literacy, comfort in a fast-paced high-volume environment, and the ability to
**coach teammates**.

### What you'll do
- Drive profitability through timely, accurate, compliant financial performance.
- **Forecast monthly with each salesperson** — customer count and type, closing
  percentages by category, time utilization, prospecting, unit sales, projected
  income.
- **Partner and coach salespeople** to maximize opportunity.
- **Conduct sales meetings.**
- **Provide on-the-job training for salespeople.**
- **Review monthly commission sheets, productivity reports, salespeople's
  forecasts, and profit performance with the Dealer** — by salesperson and as a
  department.
- Ensure legal and financial paperwork is accurate, timely, and organized.

### What they're looking for
- Strong automotive sales and finance experience
- **Experience in a dealership management position**
- Be transparent · take the high road · willingness to learn · take ownership ·
  celebrate small successes

# Fit analysis

## This is the best-matched posting so far
The role is roughly half F&I desk, half **sales-floor coaching and
development** — and the coaching half is the part of Santos's record we have
had the least room to use elsewhere. He supported and trained a 15-person sales
floor for 28 years on deal structure, disclosure, and the finance hand-off.
Their job description is that work, written out.

## The two real gaps

1. **Orlando — resolved, and it is now an asset.** He lives in Miami, ~3.5
   hours away, so this is a relocation. Confirmed 2026-09-07: **his son,
   daughter-in-law, and granddaughter live in the Orlando area** and he wants
   the move for his own reasons.

   This is worth more than a willingness statement. The hiring manager's real
   worry about any relocator is that they leave within a year; family in the
   market answers it before it is asked. The letter leads with it.

2. **EchoPark is a used-vehicle, one-price ("no-haggle") brand**, not a
   franchise new-car store. Consequences:
   - No manufacturer F&I product line — his Nissan Security Plus Platinum and
     Award of Excellence are evidence of *performance*, not transferable brand
     credentials. The letter uses them as proof of numbers, not as pedigree.
   - One-price selling changes the finance conversation. Worth him reading up
     on the EchoPark model before an interview.
   - Lender mix will be broader/deeper subprime than a Nissan store. His
     full-credit-spectrum experience is directly relevant here — arguably more
     so than at a luxury store.

3. **LinkedIn flagged the application as missing required qualifications**
   (low fit). Most likely the location mismatch. Worth noting that the
   algorithm is already down-ranking him, which makes the letter matter more.

## Angle chosen for the letter
Lead with the coaching, because it is what they named the job after and it is
genuinely his. Then the opening-team argument: a store opening in December
needs someone who can set the finance process on day one rather than learn it.
Address relocation plainly and early so it is not a question mark. Bilingual is
a real asset in Orlando's market and gets a line.

## Not claimed
CRM proficiency, external audit experience, formal internal compliance-review
ownership, any used-car or one-price retail experience.
